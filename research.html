Fee elasticity of benign flow (TSLA pair, closed-market phases, 7 days)

Observed pool: 4.0bp average fee, $17.7M volume. Control: 35.0bp, $4.9M. Volume ratio 3.6x for a fee ratio of 8.7x, so elasticity is roughly 0.6: cutting the fee 8.7x bought 3.6x the volume and about a third of the revenue. Crude (two pools, different depth and routing), but it says the competitor's 0.04% closed-market fee gives revenue away; a fee near the default earns more from the same benign flow.

Policy backtest (rules decider, time-of-day only, 7 days)

npm run replay -- --days 7 --policy. The rules decider as of 2026-09-08: default 0.30%; 1.50% in the last 60 minutes before the open; 1.20% in the first 60 minutes after it; 0.30% otherwise (no gap, vol or earnings inputs in the backtest). Flow model: informed swaps (30-minute markout above the fee they paid) trade only if their edge still beats the new fee; uninformed volume scales with (old fee / new fee)^0.6.

What it says

  1. Toxic flow is concentrated when the underlying is moving: pre-open, the open, and above all the first 30 minutes after it. Across three tickers and five pools: first-30m markout 14-59bp, open 6-28bp, pre 3-12bp. At 0.35% the static pools keep most of their fee; the dynamic pools, which charged 0.03-0.40%, lost money in pre and open hours on every ticker.
  2. Closed-market flow was benign this week. Weekend, holiday and post-close markout is 0.2-3bp on every pool, and it was 40-90% of the dynamic pools' volume. The competitor prices it at 0.04% and attracts several times the static pool's volume, but with elasticity ~0.6 the static pool still earned more fees from that flow. Benign does not mean it should be nearly free.
  3. Caveat on the closed-market result, partly retired. The 14-day check adds an ordinary weekend with the same benign result. The remaining caveat is a weekend with material news (Friday after-hours earnings, a weekend headline): the closure itself was quiet in this sample; the gap is only realized once the underlying reopens, which is exactly where the toxicity shows up (pre and first-30m). A weekend that contains news (Friday after-hours earnings, a weekend headline) would move the reference and could make the weekend toxic. The gap between the pool price and the Chainlink reference is the direct measure and should dominate over "market closed" as a signal.
  4. The what-if schedule (0.30% open, 1.20% last hour before open, 0.60% closed) with volume held constant is an upper bound and not a forecast; the competitor's fee is a live demonstration that flow is fee-elastic when it is benign.

Consequences for Margin

  • Raise the fee for: pre-open (especially the last 60 minutes), the first 30-60 minutes after the open, any tick where |gap| is large, and around earnings. Not for "weekend" as such.
  • Around the default during long closures while the reference is not moving: the flow is benign, moderately fee-sensitive, and pays best near the default.
  • Treat gapPct and reference-feed staleness as first-class inputs; the Chainlink stock feeds update on 0.5% deviation, 24/5.
  • Re-run after a weekend that contains news, and for a second ticker, before hardcoding anything.
